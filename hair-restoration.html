Hair Loss & Alopecia

Androgenetic Alopecia

Also known as male or female pattern hair loss, this is one of the most common causes of progressive hair thinning. It typically presents as gradual reduction in hair density and may affect the frontal hairline, crown or overall scalp coverage. Includes Male Pattern Hair Loss, Female Pattern Hair Loss, Receding Hairline, and Crown & Vertex Thinning.

Hair Shedding

Telogen & Anagen Effluvium

Forms of excessive or rapid hair shedding that can occur following illness, significant stress, weight loss, nutritional deficiencies, hormonal changes, pregnancy, menopause, or medical treatments. Characterised by diffuse hair thinning across the scalp.

Localised & Tension Loss

Alopecia Areata & Traction Alopecia

Alopecia Areata is an autoimmune condition causing sudden, localised areas of hair loss across the scalp, eyebrows, or body. Traction Alopecia results from repeated tension from tight hairstyles or extensions. Also assesses Trichotillomania and Pressure Alopecia.

Scarring & Inflammatory

Cicatricial Alopecia

Inflammatory scarring hair loss conditions including Central Centrifugal Cicatricial Alopecia (CCCA), Frontal Fibrosing Alopecia (FFA), Lichen Planopilaris (LPP), and Discoid Lupus Erythematosus (DLE). Early assessment is vital as referral to a dermatologist may be required.

Important: Sudden, patchy, painful, inflamed or unexplained hair loss may require assessment by a GP or dermatologist. Website information and photographs cannot provide a diagnosis.
